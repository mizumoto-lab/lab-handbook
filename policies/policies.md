# Mizumoto Lab Policies

Last updated: 2026-08-05

Next review: 2027-08

## Purpose

This document records the core expectations of the Mizumoto Lab.

These policies apply to graduate students, undergraduate researchers, postdoctoral researchers, and long-term visitors. Individual appointment letters, course requirements, project agreements, departmental rules, and Auburn University policies may establish additional requirements.

## Core principles

Our research often requires handling and killing insects. We should make responsible use of every organism by working carefully, documenting our observations, and communicating our findings honestly.

All lab members are expected to:

- Work on research that they can be proud of.
- Work carefully and strategically rather than rushing.
- Report mistakes, unexpected results, and uncertainty honestly.
- Support lab members and treat science as a collaborative activity.
- Respect differences in background, identity, culture, beliefs, working style, experience, and personality.
- Protect their own health and well-being and seek help when needed.
- Raise tension, hostility, unsafe practices, or other concerns rather than allowing them to continue.

### What lab members can expect from Nobu

Lab members can expect Nobu to:

- Explain the scientific direction of the lab and the goals of their projects.
- Provide research guidance and advice on papers, presentations, proposals, methods, analysis, and professional development.
- Make reasonable efforts to provide the resources needed for agreed research activities.
- Provide feedback within an agreed time frame.
- Discuss expectations, project ownership, and publication credit openly.
- Support career development through mentoring, introductions, recommendation letters, nominations, and opportunities appropriate to each member's goals.
- Be open to constructive feedback about the lab environment and his mentoring.
- Take concerns about physical and emotional well-being seriously.
- Continue improving his communication and mentoring practices.

### What Nobu expects from lab members

Nobu expects lab members to:

- Communicate regularly about progress, problems, absences, and changes that may affect their work.
- Ask for feedback and assistance before a small problem becomes a major problem.
- Participate reliably in agreed meetings, research activities, and shared responsibilities.
- Treat other lab members respectfully and contribute to a constructive environment.
- Use lab time, animals, equipment, funds, and shared resources responsibly.
- Keep research records, data, code, and project files organized and accessible.
- Meet agreed deadlines or communicate early when a deadline is no longer realistic.
- Discuss outside or side projects when they involve lab time, data, organisms, equipment, funding, or collaborators.
- Follow all relevant safety, research integrity, university, and legal requirements.

### Appointments, compensation, and workload

Paid lab members should receive written terms describing their appointment, compensation, workload, and responsibilities. Appointment letters and Auburn University policies take precedence over this policy.

Compensation reflects university and departmental requirements, funding, experience, responsibilities, performance, and internal equity. Rates and graduate stipends will be reviewed at least annually and when appointments, responsibilities, or funding availability change, but an increase is not guaranteed. Please see [current rates](compensation-rates.md).

I expect graduate students to be familiar with their own degree program, including the Calendar, Checklist, and Requirements. Read and bookmark this [resource](https://graduate.auburn.edu/current-students/academic-resources/on-track-graduation.php?utm_source=graduate&utm_medium=web).

### Graduate research assistantships

Graduate students supported through the Mizumoto Lab are normally appointed as Graduate Research Assistants (GRA). GRA represents an appointment of 0.33 FTEs. A 0.33 FTE assistantship corresponds to approximately 13 hours per week of assigned research responsibilities. These responsibilities may overlap substantially with the student's thesis or dissertation research and may include agreed contributions to shared lab work. The lab does not routinely track these hours; concerns will be discussed if assigned responsibilities are not being met.

### Hourly paid undergraduate

Paid undergraduate work on defined tasks that support lab research, insect care, data collection, data processing, or other agreed lab activities. The scope of work, expected weekly hours, and primary supervisor should be established when the appointment begins. Hourly employment is normally a supporting role within a project rather than independently leading the project. All hours worked must be recorded accurately using Auburn's required timekeeping system. Required job-related meetings and training count as work time when they are part of the student's assigned responsibilities.

A day-to-day supervisor may be a graduate student, postdoctoral researcher, or other experienced lab member. The day-to-day supervisor provides training, assigns routine tasks, reviews the student's work, and communicates significant problems or changes to Nobu. Nobu remains responsible for the student's appointment and the overall scientific direction of the work.

## Communication, presence, and flexibility

### Regular communication matters more than a rigid schedule

Lab members do not need permission for every change in their daily schedule, occasional remote work, or time away. However, they should notify Nobu when an absence or schedule change affects meetings, deadlines, shared work, or their availability for an extended period.

Current meeting schedules and expectations for in-person work are discussed at the beginning of each semester and may differ among roles and projects.

When requesting feedback on a manuscript, proposal, presentation, or other substantial document:

- State the external deadline.
- Propose an internal deadline, normally at least one week earlier.
- Provide enough time for meaningful review whenever possible.
- Send a reminder if no response is received within two working days or as the deadline approaches.

Nobu usually writes in the morning (7:00-9:00 am) if there is no other schedule. Except for this time, Nobu is available for any kind of meeting with lab members until 5:00 pm. 

If lab members email me, I try to reply on the same or the next day if it is within a usual working day.

Please set a deadline whenever you request feedback for your proposal or manuscript. It will help both of us. And feel free to bug me close to the deadlines (even on the day of) -- I won't be offended by reminders. Instead, I will appreciate them!
 
I always use a virtual deadline one week before for any tasks with a hard deadline. This is for safety. I may get sick or have an accident. Please remember this and behave in the same way. But in an emergency, do not hesitate to ask me to do something in the last second.

The Mizumoto Lab Microsoft 365 group or Team chat are used for announcements and messages relevant to all lab members.

Email: mizumoto_lab@groups.auburn.edu

Use the group address for information that should reach the entire lab. Project-specific questions and discussions should normally be sent only to the people involved.


### Meetings

- Biweekly Lab Meeting
  - The lab meeting provides a regular space for sharing information, discussing ongoing research, and learning from one another. In the following order:
    1. Lab logistics and announcements: Share information, upcoming events, needs, or decisions relevant to the lab.
    2. Brief individual updates: Each person briefly describes:
       - What they are currently working on
       - Any obstacles or questions
       - Their next step
    3. Focal discussion: One person leads a discussion on a useful topic, such as:
       - Ongoing research or preliminary results
       - An experimental or analytical question
       - A new idea
       - A paper or method
       - A practice presentation
    4. Next meeting: Decide the focal person and tentative topic for the next meeting.
  - Members will generally take turns leading the focal discussion, but the schedule can remain flexible depending on current needs.
- Department seminar
  - The Department of Entomology & Plant Pathology has a department seminar during semesters; you are expected to attend it. Ask a question even if it differs greatly from our field. Thinking about what to ask for a question is the most effective way to attend seminars.
- Shared Lab Meeting
  - We have shared lab meetings between Penick Lab (Entomology), Mizumoto Lab (Entomology), and Smith Lab (Biology) on collective behavior in social insects. This is a monthly meeting on the first week of Friday.
- Annual graduate student review
  - Graduate students complete the annual-review process described [here](../annual_review/README.md).

These meetings are normally held during the semester. Graduate students and postdoctoral researchers are expected to attend unless excused or the meeting is not relevant to their appointment. Undergraduate researchers are welcome but attendance is optional unless participation has been agreed upon as part of their research responsibilities.

## Research integrity

Honest mistakes are part of research. Hiding, altering, or failing to report a mistake is a more serious problem than making one.

Lab members must not fabricate, falsify, or selectively omit data; misrepresent methods or contributions; plagiarize; or otherwise engage in research misconduct.

Unexpected observations, failed experiments, exclusions, data-processing decisions, and changes from the original plan must be documented. When uncertain about an appropriate practice, ask before proceeding.

### Safety and required training

Lab members must take all the required training. Detailed instruction will be maintained in the onboarding checklist.
Some training requires recurrence. Take it on-time if you get notification.

### Authorship and contributions

Authorship and author order should reflect substantial scholarly contributions and responsibility for the work. Funding, supervision, employment, access to equipment, providing routine assistance, or supplying materials alone do not automatically establish authorship.

At the beginning of a project, and again as the project develops, the people involved should discuss:

- The purpose and ownership of the project.
- Expected contributions.
- Who is likely to lead the work.
- Preliminary expectations for authorship and author order.
- Responsibilities for data collection, analysis, interpretation, writing, and revision.

These expectations may change as actual contributions change.

For projects led by the Mizumoto Lab:

- A trainee who leads the project and makes the major contributions is normally expected to be first author.
- Nobu is normally the senior author when his contributions justify authorship.
- Other authors and their order are determined by their contributions.
- All authors must review and approve the final work and take responsibility for their contributions.
- If leadership of an unfinished project is transferred, first authorship may also change after discussion.
- Co-first authorship may be used when contributions justify it.

Routine technical work may develop into an authorship-level contribution when a person becomes substantially involved in study design, interpretation, analysis, or writing. These possibilities should be discussed openly rather than assumed.

Abstracts, manuscripts, posters, talks, and public research products based on lab work must be reviewed by the relevant project leaders and coauthors before submission or presentation.

## Data management

Research conducted in the lab must remain accessible to the lab and reproducible by another researcher.

Each project must have:

- A project location on Auburn-approved storage (i.e., OneDrive, Box), shared with Nobu, with the structure of
- A GitHub repository when version control is appropriate.
- A README describing the project, responsible people, file organization, and how to reproduce the main analyses.
- Clearly separated raw data, processed data, code, outputs, and manuscript files.
- At least one backup independent of the primary working copy.
- Documentation of study design, experimental conditions, sample identities, exclusions, unusual events, and data-processing decisions.

Raw data must not be overwritten. Corrections and processing should produce new files or be reproducibly generated by code.

Large videos and other files unsuitable for GitHub must be stored in the designated lab storage system. The repository should document where those files are stored and how they relate to the analysis.

Passwords, confidential student information, protected university data, and other sensitive information must not be stored in project repositories.

### Repository and project ownership

Repositories for research performed as part of the Mizumoto Lab should be created under the lab's GitHub organization rather than under a trainee's personal account.

This ensures that:

- The lab retains access when a student graduates or leaves.
- Project history and contributions remain visible.
- Access can be added or removed without changing repository ownership.
- Work can be transferred to another student without copying or recreating the repository.

Students retain credit for their contributions through the Git history, authorship, presentations, and other research products. Repository ownership is an administrative arrangement and does not determine scholarly credit.

A genuinely independent project that does not use lab time, data, organisms, funding, equipment, or other resources may remain in a personal repository. This should be discussed when the project begins.

### Project continuity and unfinished work

Research data should not remain inaccessible or indefinitely unpublished because a person has left the lab or stopped working on a project.

If a project becomes inactive, Nobu and the project members will discuss:

- Whether the original researcher wishes to continue.
- A realistic timeline and next milestone.
- Whether leadership or specific tasks should be transferred.
- How prior and future contributions will be credited.
- The publication and data-release plan.

Reassignment does not erase a person's completed contributions. Authorship and acknowledgment will be based on the contribution to the final product and the responsibilities accepted by each contributor.

## Professional development

Training should reflect each member's role, experience, and goals. Potential areas of development include:

- Handling and maintaining study organisms.
- Experimental design and careful observation.
- Data management and reproducible research.
- Statistical analysis in R or other appropriate tools.
- Image and video analysis in Python or other appropriate tools.
- Command-line and version-control skills.
- Scientific writing and visualization.
- Oral, poster, classroom, and public communication.
- Time management and meeting deadlines.
- Collaboration, mentoring, and project leadership.

No member is expected to arrive with all of these skills. Training priorities should be discussed during onboarding and reviewed as the project develops.

## Raising concerns and resolving conflict

When possible and safe, lab members are encouraged to address ordinary misunderstandings directly and respectfully.

A concern may be raised with Nobu at any time. If the concern involves Nobu, cannot be resolved within the lab, or would be inappropriate to discuss with him, members should contact another trusted faculty member, the graduate program leadership, the department chair, or an appropriate Auburn University support or reporting office.

## Maintaining these policies

These policies should be reviewed with each new member rather than assigned as passive reading.

The lab will review them at least once each August. Members are encouraged to open a GitHub issue or propose an edit whenever a policy is outdated, confusing, missing, or inconsistent with actual lab practice.
