# Mizumoto Lab Compensation Rates

Last updated: 2026-08-05

Next review: 2027-08

This document records current compensation rates and lab-funded incentives. Individual appointment letters state the actual appointment period, stipend or hourly rate, and other applicable terms. All compensation is subject to Auburn University, Graduate School, departmental, payroll, tax, grant, and sponsor requirements.

## Graduate research assistantships

Graduate research assistantship base rates are set by the department. The standard appointment is normally 0.33 FTE.

| Degree program | Current departmental base | Fringe benefit |
|---|---:|---:|
| MS | $23,000 | 3.80% |
| PhD | $25,000 | 3.80% |

Students are responsible for required university and health-related fees.

### Lab-funded stipend levels

When funding and university rules permit, the lab may provide a stipend above the departmental base. Stipend levels should be reviewed annually, before the next academic-year appointment. It is based on demonstrated performance during the preceding year and is not automatic based on time in the program or completion of a required degree milestone. The review considers the quality and consistency of research progress, increasing independence, research communication, reliability, and contributions to the lab. Relevant evidence may include:
- Designing and completing well-planned research.
- Collecting, organizing, analyzing, or interpreting substantial data.
- Preparing or submitting a manuscript.
- Presenting research at a professional meeting.
- Preparing a fellowship or research-grant application.
- Developing reproducible protocols, datasets, code, or analytical systems.
- Successfully resolving important conceptual or technical problems.

No single activity guarantees an increase. Publication acceptance, grant success, and other outcomes influenced by external review will be considered, but students will not be penalized solely because a manuscript or proposal remains under review.

Because an MS program typically lasts about two years, students will ordinarily have one opportunity to advance.

#### PhD students

| Level | Lab supplement | Current total |
|---|---:|---:|
| PhD Level 1 | None | $25,000 |
| PhD Level 2 | +$2,000 | $27,000 |
| PhD Level 3 | +$4,000 | $29,000 |

**PhD Level 2:** Following an annual merit review demonstrating strong and sustained research progress, increasing intellectual and technical independence, and meaningful research outputs. Evidence may include manuscripts, conference presentations, fellowship or grant applications, and other significant research achievements.

**PhD Level 3:** Following an annual merit review demonstrating the expectations of Level 2 and a substantial continuing contribution to the lab beyond the student's own research. Examples include coordinating major shared projects, training junior researchers, developing new methods, or improving important lab-wide systems.

## Graduate teaching supplements

The department may occasionally provide a $1,000-per-semester GTA supplement for additional teaching work. These supplements must be approved by the department chair and are provided through the student's university account.

## Productivity incentives for Graduate students

Research writing and grant development are important parts of graduate training. Subject to university rules and available funding, the lab provides the following one-time incentives.

### Refereed journal submission

For the first submission of each paper co-authored with Nobu Mizumoto:

- First author or co-first author: $500
- Other co-author: $200

### Refereed journal acceptance

- First author of a paper accepted by a journal designated as **incentive level 1** in the [journal list](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1tNzj1UMuvO9vShVRI0QanOSFvRSSd8Q5cm1ndOqWjYg/edit?gid=1450194090#gid=1450194090): an additional $500.

### Research grants

Students are encouraged to pursue appropriate grant-writing opportunities as professional development.

For an awarded research grant led by a student, the lab will seek to provide a one-time incentive equal to 10% of the grant income. For example, a $2,500 grant would produce a $250 incentive. Nobu does not need to be a co-author or co-applicant.

The award must be permitted by the grant, university, department, and payroll rules. Some grants cannot be used for student compensation, and graduate-school limits may restrict the amount that can be added to a student's stipend.

### Other achievements

Students may ask Nobu to consider a noteworthy research or professional achievement not covered above. Any additional incentive will be evaluated individually and added to this document if it becomes a recurring policy.

## Undergraduate research assistants

Undergraduate research assistants normally begin at **$12 per hour**. Rates may be adjusted based on experience, responsibilities, funding, and internal equity.

Paid undergraduate assistants are normally expected to work at least 10 hours per week so that they can interact regularly with the lab and make sustained progress. Lab meetings and training in tools directly required for assigned work count as paid working time. All working time must be recorded accurately using Tiger Time.
